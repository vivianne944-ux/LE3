A. Lipoproteins

Chylomicrons

  • Formed in the intestines
  • Carry the dietary fat that we ingest and bring them to the different peripheral tissues to deposit fatty acids
  • Also carry unesterified cholesterol and cholesteryl esters
  • In the capillaries, there is an enzyme called lipoprotein lipase
    • Triglycerides are removed through lipoprotein lipase in extrahepatic tissues
      • Fatty acids are deposited in adipose tissue
      • Triglycerides are deposited to peripheral tissue for storage and energy production
      • Remnants are taken up by the liver

VLDL

  • Found and produced in the liver
  • Contains triglycerides and cholesterol for distribution to the body
    • Once it loses triglycerides, it would contain more cholesterol
  • Exports triglycerides to peripheral tissues
  • Hydrolyzed by lipoprotein lipase yielding free fatty acids for storage in adipose tissue and for oxidation in cardiac muscle
    • Remnants yield LDL

LDL

  • "Bad cholesterol" as it deposits cholesterol into the lining of our arteries sometimes
  • Most cholesterol content, very small triacylglycerol content
  • Brings cholesterol to the peripheral tissues (e.g., adrenal glands and gonads) for steroid hormone production
  • Very little LDL transports cholesterol back to the liver
    • Cholesteryl esters from LDL yield free cholesterol for the synthesis of cell membranes and fat-dissolving bile acids

HDL

  • "Good cholesterol"
  • Anti-atherogenic: prevents excess cholesterol from being deposited into the arteries
  • Secreted by the liver and small intestine
  • Acquires cholesterol from peripheral tissues and transports them back to the liver
    • Protects the cholesterol homeostasis of cells, so it balances what LDL does

B. Metabolism of Lipoproteins of Hepatic Origin p.2

Hepatic Pathway

1. Acetyl CoA is changed into mevalonic acid via HMG-CoA reductase (rate-limiting step)
→
2. Mevalonic acid turns into cholesterol, consumed by the lysosome, packaged in golgi vesicles, secreted as B-100 VLDL
→
3. VLDL goes into the capillary endothelium. Lipoprotein lipase there lets VLDL deposit free fatty acid for energy production or β-oxidation
4. Once fatty acids are deposited it turns to LDL (mostly cholesterol), transported to the glands (adrenal, testis) for steroid hormones
→
5. Remnants / excess LDL return to the liver, so excess cholesterol is used again to make VLDL
→
6. HDL carries some cholesterol and returns it back to the liver. Prevents too much cholesterol from reaching peripheral cells

II. Pathophysiology of Dyslipidemia p.2-3

Definition

Dyslipidemia or hyperlipoproteinemia refers to metabolic disorders that involve elevations in any lipoprotein species.

  • Lipoproteins that contain (apo) B-100 convey lipids into the artery wall: VLDL, IDL, & LDL

Major Clinical Sequelae

SequelaKey points
Acute pancreatitisMostly high triglyceride levels
AtherosclerosisFat deposits in capillaries or arteries
Can lead to ischemic heart disease and cerebrovascular disease, the top 2 leading causes of death in the Philippines
Coronary artery deposit → narrows the artery → ischemia → heart attack
Carotid artery deposit → impedes blood flow to the brain → ischemic stroke
Endpoint: infarction or ischemia

Causes and Risk

  • Dyslipidemia can be caused by:
    • High consumption of fatty food
    • Sedentary lifestyle
    • Inherited gene defect in lipoprotein metabolism
    • Combination of two or more factors
  • Dyslipidemia, coupled with hypertension, diabetes, and tobacco smoking, can increase the risk of coronary disease, ischemic stroke, and peripheral arterial occlusive disease

A. HMG-CoA Reductase Inhibitors (Statins) p.3-5

Drugs

  • Atorvastatin**, Rosuvastatin**, Simvastatin**
    • ** Found in the Philippine Drug Formulary
  • Lovastatin, Pravastatin, Fluvastatin, Pitavastatin
    • Also available in the Philippines and included in the Clinical Practice Guidelines for Dyslipidemia

Mechanism

  • They competitively bind to the active site of HMG-CoA reductase, preventing the conversion of HMG-CoA to mevalonate
    • Mevalonate is a precursor molecule necessary for the synthesis of cholesterol, as well as other isoprenoids
    • HMG CoA reductase inhibitors are structural analogs
  • Competitive inhibition results in a decrease in cholesterol production
    • This is sensed by the liver, causing an increase in expression of LDL receptors on the surface of hepatocytes
    • Promoting uptake of LDL from the blood

1. Inhibition of HMG CoA reductase = decreased concentration of cholesterol within the cell

→
2. Low intracellular cholesterol stimulates the synthesis of LDL receptors
→
3. Increased number of LDL receptors promotes uptake of LDL from blood back to the liver
→
4. Low intracellular cholesterol decreases the secretion of VLDL

Adverse Effects and Toxicity Must Know p.5

Mnemonic: HMG CoA R-I
  • Hepatotoxicity
    • Liver enzymes may be elevated more than 3x the normal (discontinue statin)
  • Myopathy
    • Myositis, myalgia
  • Gastrointestinal upset
    • Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea
  • Cataracts
  • Arthralgia
  • Rhabdomyolysis
  • Increased risk of diabetes
    • Fasting plasma glucose increase 5-7 mg/dL

📋 Formative quiz rationale: statin-induced myalgia / muscle injury is associated with increased creatine kinase (CK).

Precautions p.5

  • Patients with:
    • Pre-existing liver disease
    • History of alcohol abuse
      • Levels of serum aminotransferase may exceed 3x normal, giving more severe reactions
  • Teratogenic: not given to pregnant or lactating women (see Statin Use in Pregnancy below)
  • Children with heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia may be treated with statins at 7 years of age, when myelination of the CNS is essentially complete
  • Drugs are not usually indicated before the age of 16 in the absence of multiple risk factors or compound genetic dyslipidemias

Statin Use in Pregnancy p.5

  • Statins were Category X according to USFDA but this was changed in 2021
  • The FDA now argues that "the benefits of statins may include prevention of serious or potentially fatal events in a small group of very high-risk patients" and "contraindicating these drugs in all pregnant women is not appropriate."
  • High-risk patients:
    • Previous atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD)
    • Familial hypercholesterolemia (FH) with markedly elevated cholesterol levels
  • Pravastatin – used in clinical trials evaluating its potential to prevent pre-eclampsia
    • It is hydrophilic and a substrate of the P-glycoprotein efflux pump, theoretically limiting its ability for placental transfer
    • Results: Pravastatin started in the 2nd and 3rd trimester is safe in pregnant women without an increase in congenital anomalies
  • Ultimately, the continuation of statins in these patients rests on shared decision-making between patient and clinician
  • Those with ASCVD events, especially recent ones, should be encouraged to continue statins during pregnancy or resume them as soon as possible if they are withheld

Clinical Question 7.2: Among individuals with ASCVD, should fibrates be given on top of statin therapy once LDL-C goal is achieved?

  • Without diabetes, not at goal LDL-C: routinely adding fibrates on top of statin therapy is NOT RECOMMENDED for primary or secondary prevention of cardiovascular disease
  • With diabetes: routinely adding fibrates on top of statin therapy is NOT RECOMMENDED for primary or secondary prevention
  • However, adding fibrates to statins may be considered among MEN with controlled diabetes, low HDL-C (<35 mg/dL) and persistently high triglycerides (>200 mg/dL) for prevention of CV disease

C. Inhibitor of Intestinal Sterol Absorption — Ezetimibe p.7

Mechanism

Selectively inhibits intestinal absorption of cholesterol and phytosterols
Target: NPC1L1 (transport protein)
→
Lowers intestinal delivery of cholesterol to the liver
→
Increases expression of hepatic LDL receptors, promoting uptake of LDL from the plasma
Effects (values) Lowers LDL-C by 18-20%
No change in triglyceride and HDL-C
Synergistic with reductase inhibitors, producing decrements as great as 25% in LDL-C beyond that achieved with reductase inhibitor alone

Indication

Used as second-line therapy combined with statins when the therapeutic goal is not achieved at the maximal tolerated statin dose, or in cases where statin cannot be prescribed.

Pharmacokinetics

  • Administered in the morning or evening, with or without food
  • Readily absorbed and conjugated in the intestine to an active glucuronide
    • Peak blood levels in 12-14 hours
  • Undergoes enterohepatic circulation
    • Half-life is 22 hrs
  • Approximately 80% excreted in the feces

Dosage

Ezetimibe: 10 mg/tablet once a day

📣 Comes in 10 mg tablets, no capsules.

Mechanism

SiteAction and result

Bile Acid Binding Resins (BA Sequestrants) p.9

Drugs

Colestipol | Cholestyramine | Colesevelam

Mechanism

  • Large polymeric cationic exchange resins that are insoluble to water
  • Bind bile acids in the intestinal lumen and prevent their absorption (increase excretion of cholesterol)
  • Bile acids
    • Metabolites of cholesterol
    • Normally efficiently reabsorbed in jejunum and ileum and brought back to the liver
    • Excretion is increased up to tenfold when resins are given
Resin binds bile acids in the lumen. Excretion increased up to tenfold
→
Enhanced conversion of cholesterol to bile acids in the liver → lowering cholesterol in the circulation
→
Compensatory increase in synthesis of LDL receptors increases the removal of LDL from the blood
Must knowOnly useful for isolated increases in LDL ❗
Producing approximately 15-30% reduction in LDL-C in maximal dosage
  • Studies also show an increase in HDL-C by 3-5%
  • Bile acid sequestrants may increase triglycerides
  • Can also reduce pruritus in patients with cholestasis and bile salt accumulation

Pharmacokinetics

  • Essentially not absorbed systemically, so only GI effects
  • Available in powder, granules, or tablets
  • Effects are more visible a month after use
  • Not expected to cause fetal harm in pregnant patients since not absorbed systemically

Dosage

DrugDose

Mostly GI related and seen in chronic use.

Mnemonic: B. ACID
  • Bloating (with long term use) and Bleeding due to Vit. K deficiency
  • Abdominal discomfort
  • Constipation
  • Indigestion
  • Decreased absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (ADEK), folic acid and other negatively charged drugs

VI. Agents Under Development p.10

A. CETP Inhibitor

  • Cholesteryl ester transfer protein (CETP)
    • Transfers cholesterol esters from mature HDL particles to triglyceride-rich lipoproteins that deliver esters to the liver, where cholesterol and bile acids can be eliminated into the intestine
  • Inhibition of CETP leads to accumulation of HDL particles and diminution of transport of cholesteryl esters to the liver
    • Some reduction of LDL-C can be achieved
    • Cholesterol efflux capacity enhanced
  • CETP inhibitors undergoing clinical trials: Torcetrapib, Anacetrapib

B. AMP Kinase Activation

  • AMP-activated protein kinase acts as a sensor of energy status in cells
  • When increased ATP is required, AMP kinase will:
    • Increase fatty acid oxidation
    • Increase insulin sensitivity
    • Inhibit cholesterol and triglyceride biosynthesis

C. Cyclodextrins

Circular sugar polymers that solubilize cholesterol from tissue sites such as atherosclerotic plaques.

Cram Sheet

Core Definitions

  • Dyslipidemia / hyperlipoproteinemia = metabolic disorders that involve elevations in any lipoprotein species
  • Apo B-100 lipoproteins convey lipids into the artery wall: VLDL, IDL, LDL
  • Sequelae: acute pancreatitis (high TG), atherosclerosis (IHD and cerebrovascular disease = top 2 causes of death in PH)
  • HMG-CoA reductase = rate-limiting step (Acetyl CoA to mevalonic acid to cholesterol)
  • LDL = "bad" (most cholesterol) · HDL = "good", anti-atherogenic · VLDL = exports TG

Common Confusions and Warnings

  • Prodrugs = lovastatin, simvastatin · active as given = atorva, fluva, rosuva
  • Statin exceptions
    • Food does not enhance absorption: pravastatin, pitavastatin
    • Not metabolized by CYP: pravastatin
    • Metabolized by CYP2C9: fluvastatin, rosuvastatin
  • Fibrates with statins: myopathy, rhabdomyolysis
    • Fenofibrate is the fibrate of choice to combine with statins
  • Fibrates potentiate anticoagulants
  • Niacin potentiates antihypertensives
  • Ezetimibe: no change in TG or HDL, not a CYP450 substrate
  • Resins bind negatively charged drugs including pravastatin, fluvastatin, ezetimibe, warfarin, digoxin
  • Pregnancy
    • Statins: Category X changed in 2021, continuation rests on shared decision-making
    • Fibrates and niacin: Category C
    • Resins: not expected to cause fetal harm
  • Fibrates + resin may increase the risk of cholelithiasis
  • Statin + resin may not control VLDL in combined hyperlipoproteinemia
